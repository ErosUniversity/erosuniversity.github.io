The story of Sun Wu Shi

The Monkey Who Stayed

Sun Wu Shi is born from the thought that no one talks about and no one wants to ask about.

There were two monkeys in the stone.

The first one burst out and light shot from his eyes all the way to heaven. You know his story. Sun Wukong, Great Sage, Equal of Heaven.

The second came out a little later, because someone had to check whether the stone was okay. It wasn’t. It was a broken stone. He sat on it for a while.

His brother learned 72 transformations. He learned to ride the clouds. Then he went down to the underworld, found the Book of Life and Death, and crossed out his own name, along with the names of all the monkeys.

The second monkey’s name was on that page too. So he walked down after him, which took years, because he could only walk. He asked for the pen and wrote his name back in.

“Why would you do that?” asked the clerk of the dead.

“I want to know how it ends,” he said.

He kept the pen. It’s the only reason anyone calls him Sun Wu Shi, the one who saw that it was real.

His brother went to war with heaven and bet the Buddha he could leap out of his hand. He flew to the end of the world, where five great pillars stood. He wrote The Great Sage was here on one of them, pissed at its foot, and flew home in triumph. The Buddha opened his hand. It was written on his finger. They put a mountain on Wukong for 500 years.

Wu Shi walked. It took him his whole life. He was old when he reached the five pillars. He read his brother’s graffiti, and the stain was still there. He took the pen from behind his ear and wrote underneath: me too.

Then he sat down against the finger and started laughing, and he couldn’t stop.

The hand trembled. The Buddha looked down.

“You know where you are?”

“Yes.”

“Then you know there is no way out.”

“Yes.”

“I could make you immortal. I could give you the emptiness your brother will find. What do you want?”

Wu Shi thought about it for a long time.

“Is there lunch?”

The Buddha laughed. Nobody had ever heard that before, and it’s why the earth still shakes now and then. When the hand was still again, the monkey was dead, because monkeys die. It was in the book.

He has no temple and no heaven. But when someone laughs on the worst day of their life, at the hospital, at the funeral, at the bank, Sun Wu Shi is there for exactly as long as the laugh lasts. Then he’s gone again.

Don’t pray to him, he won’t answer. He’s eating.
